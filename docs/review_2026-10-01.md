## 1. NEW — the far field and R_S use a source form that is wrong for a periodic box

### 1.1 What the literature says [cited]

Sarkar & Hussaini (1993), NASA CR-191543 / ICASE 93-74, is the standard reference for computing
Lighthill sound from turbulence in a periodic box. They write the far field in two forms:

- **Eq. (1), space-derivative form:** p = (1/4πc₀²) ∫ ∂ᵢ∂ⱼTᵢⱼ(y, t − r/c₀)/r dy.
- **Eq. (2), time-derivative form:** p ≈ xᵢxⱼ/(4πc₀²|x|³) ∫ T̈ᵢⱼ(y, t − r/c₀) dy.

Their verdict on Eq. (1), verbatim:

> "Eq. (1) is not quite appropriate here because of the periodic boundary conditions used for
> the turbulence simulation. Because of the non-zero velocity at the fluid boundary of the
> periodic box, the dipole contribution to the sound from the surface of the flow domain is
> non-zero and would be included in a calculation using Eq. (1). The surface contribution is an
> artifact of the boundary conditions and is not of interest to us. Eq. (2), on the other hand,
> gives directly the quadrupole sound characteristic of a finite volume of turbulent flow
> embedded in a medium at rest."

They also require "the length of the computational domain much larger than the spatial
correlation lengths."

### 1.2 What silent-field does [code]

- `source_timeseries(..., form="divergence")` (`src/acoustics/lighthill.py:108–124`) builds
  S = ∂ᵢ∂ⱼ(uᵢuⱼ). This is the space-derivative form.
- `scripts/03_compute_sources.py` radiates S with `far_field_pressure`
  (`src/acoustics/greens.py:218`).
- R_S, the "most robust number" in HANDOFF, is the cone share of S.
- `far_field_pressure_ibp` (`greens.py:270`) puts ∂ᵢ∂ⱼ on the Green's function and integrates
  against Tᵢⱼ. That is the 2D frequency-domain analogue of Eq. (2). It was written for a
  different reason: to avoid differentiating a noisy surrogate field twice. Nothing calls it.

### 1.3 Measured on the truth data [computed]

**Compact term.** On a periodic grid the k = 0 mode of S is identically zero, but the box
integral of Tᵢⱼ is not:

Reading:
- **The S-form far field is mostly artifact at every frequency.** That includes the Tier-0 band
  ω ≥ c₀·dk = 11.3, where it is 16–20× too large. A Stage-3 far-field error computed this
  way would mostly compare the boundary artifacts of truth and prediction.
- **The excess falls with frequency.** S/T falls roughly as 1/k at low ω, then levels off. A
  surface term radiating as a dipole (∝ k) against a volume quadrupole (∝ k²) would produce
  that pattern. This is consistent with Sarkar & Hussaini's diagnosis, though not a proof of
  it.
- **Below the first box mode, the physical radiation is the compact term.** It accounts for
  68–97% of the T-form far field. This is exactly what the S-based cone metric drops, because
  S has no k = 0 content.
- **This explains why R depends on box size.** A bigger box moves radiation from the
  unrepresentable gap (0, dk) onto grid modes. Before this finding, the only explanation on
  record was "cone energy sits in the largest box mode."

## 2. Stage 4 (Sobolev) — the post-Tier-0 conclusion needs reframing

### 2.1 The default λ makes the Sobolev term negligible everywhere [computed]

From `figures/06_sobolev_argument.json` at M = 0.1: the cone share divided by L2 is 0.99974
at λ = 1e-2. Since the weight inside the cone is ≈ 1, this ratio is
≈ 1/(1 + λ·r·⟨w̃⟩_P), so the Sobolev term is ≈ **0.03% of the total objective** under the data
spectrum, and ~1–2% even for spectrally white error. At the default λ the objective is L2
everywhere, not only in the cone. A referee will read "inert at λ = 1e-2" as "you switched
Sobolev training off."

Where the term does matter, it lowers the cone share:

| λ | 1e-2 | 1 | 10 | 1e2 | 1e3 | → ∞ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| share ÷ L2 | 1.000 | 0.974 | 0.794 | 0.303 | 0.086 | 0.053 |

`HANDOFF.md:57` says "identical to L2 … for 100× the default λ". At 100× (λ = 1) the ratio is
0.974, so "within 3%" is accurate and "identical" is not.

### 2.2 The claim that is robust

w_eff ≥ 1 everywhere and ≈ 1 in the cone, so **Sobolev weighting can never raise the cone
share, and wherever it is strong enough to change training it lowers it by up to ~19×.**
This holds for any error spectrum and any λ. The old "~10–19× worse" number was the strong-λ
end of this sweep, not a pure error; the error was attributing it to λ = 1e-2.

### 2.3 The standard practitioner loss is the multiplicative one [cited]

neuraloperator's `H1Loss` (`neuralop/losses/data_losses.py`) computes, verbatim:

```python
diff = torch.sum((dict_x[0] - dict_y[0]) ** 2, ...)
for j in range(1, self.d + 1):
    diff += torch.sum((dict_x[j] - dict_y[j]) ** 2, ...)
```

That is ‖e‖² + ‖∇e‖² with unit weight, divided by the same norm of the target in the relative
version. In Fourier space this is (1 + k²) up to finite-difference error, i.e. the
multiplicative reading. The repo's additive λ = 1e-2 objective is a non-standard,
nearly-L2 variant. **Use the standard form for any trained Stage-4 run.**

### 2.4 Wrong citation [cited]

Czarnecki et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.04859, defines Sobolev training as "incorporating these
target derivatives in addition to the target values", i.e. derivatives with respect to the
network's inputs. It is not a spectral H¹ field loss. Cite H¹ losses as used with neural
operators instead, e.g. the neuraloperator `H1Loss` above.

### 2.5 Bug: `additive_sobolev_weight` is 2× too large for kx ≠ 0 modes [computed]

- **Where:** `src/acoustics/radiating.py:119` uses `ratio = n/(n//2+1)` (N_cells / N_half).
- **What it misses:** each interior rfft2 half-plane coefficient stands for two full-plane
  modes (+k and −k). Per full-plane mode the correct ratio is n/(n+2), half of what is used,
  for every mode with kx ≠ 0.
- **Measured against `losses.sobolev_loss` in torch** (single cosine modes, n = 64, λ = 0.37):

  | mode (kx, ky) | measured w_eff − 1 | analysis w_eff − 1 | ratio |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | (3, 5) | 1.822e-02 | 3.644e-02 | **2.000** |
  | (0, 4) | 1.770e-02 | 1.770e-02 | 1.000 |
  | (7, 0) | 2.603e-02 | 5.206e-02 | **2.000** |

- **Why the tests miss it:** `tests/test_radiating.py:100` rebuilds the same ratio instead of
  evaluating the loss, so the test is circular. Replace it with a test that calls
  `sobolev_loss` on single Fourier modes, like the table above.
- **Side note:** the trained loss is anisotropic. Modes on the kx = 0 column get twice the
  Sobolev weight of other modes at the same |k|. This is an artifact of applying a half-plane
  mean without Hermitian weights in `losses.py`.
- **Impact:** the error overstates the Sobolev effect, so no conclusion flips. The λ axis of
  the sweep shifts by 2×.

## 3. Mach scaling — retire M⁸ for the right reason

### 3.1 The compactness argument is inverted [cited]

`HANDOFF.md:48` and `branch_analysis.md` §4 say compactness ωL/c₀ ~ M "breaks" at low Mach.
Schram (POLKA lecture, p. 22) states: "Acoustical compactness expressed by Helmholtz number:
He = 2fD/c₀ … Acoustical compactness depends on Mach number: He = 2Sr U/c₀ = 2Sr M." A source
is compact when He ≪ 1. Since He ∝ M, **compactness improves as M → 0**. Low Mach is the
compact regime, not the regime where compactness fails.

### 3.2 The 2D benchmark is M⁷, not M⁸ [cited]

Schoder et al., arXiv:2608.19236: "the M⁷ power law expected for compact quadrupole sources in
two dimensions (Ffowcs Williams 1969)". M⁸ (U⁸) is the 3D law.

### 3.3 But steep exponents are not, by themselves, proof of an artifact [cited]

The same paper reports, for forced 2D mixing layers at M = 0.2–0.4, that radiated power
"grows more steeply than the M⁷ law, with an effective slope decreasing as the Mach number
increases". Steeper-than-compact slopes occur in real 2D flows too. In that study, though,
Reynolds number co-varies with Mach number.

### 3.4 What to write instead

Comparing R(M), an energy fraction of a box-mode spectrum, with a radiated-power law is a
category error. Compare a radiated power computed with the Eq. (2) form (§1) against M⁷.
Only then are the exponents interpretable.

**Reading B correctly:**
- **"B ≥ 1" is not a theorem.** A model can be relatively better inside the cone than outside
  it. The predicted "B ~ O(1e6)" assumes white error; it is not a prediction for a trained
  model.
- **The training loss has no frequency axis.** `mse_loss` and `sobolev_loss` act on single
  snapshots; the model is trained on one-step pairs [code: `src/models/train.py`,
  `scripts/02`]. The loss therefore cannot favour or neglect high ω. The cone at M = 0.1 is
  the high-ω tail of the |k| = 1 shell: that shell holds **95%** of the velocity cone energy
  [computed] and **4.3%** of total energy [computed].
- **Hypothesis to test, not established [unverified]:** step-to-step incoherence (jitter) in
  teacher-forced one-step predictions at large scales lands directly in the cone. A large B
  could measure that rather than "blindness by energy weighting". Stage 3 must include two
  controls:
  1. teacher-forced one-step predictions vs free rollout;
  2. a random-error baseline with matched amplitude.
- **Compute B on the source too (B_S), using the Tᵢⱼ form (§1).** Sound comes from the source,
  and the source is quadratic in velocity. Its radiating content comes from pairs of
  energetic velocity modes that are not in the velocity cone, so velocity-B alone misses the
  mechanism.

### 4.3 §6F — "a strictly periodic source has no far field (Rayleigh's theorem)"

- **No source supports this.** Periodic structures do radiate: in the Rayleigh–Bloch
  expansion, orders with |κₙ| ≤ ω propagate (the "Bragg diffraction orders") and the rest are
  evanescent (Barnett & Greengard, quasi-periodic scattering in 2D) [cited]. That
  propagation condition is the cone condition itself.
- **The actual problem** is the surface-dipole artifact of the space-derivative form in a
  periodic box (§1.1, Sarkar & Hussaini) [cited].
- **The missing option:** (iv) use the time-derivative (Tᵢⱼ) form, which is already
  implemented as `far_field_pressure_ibp`.
- **If option (i), a tapered sub-window, is ever used:** source truncation produces spurious
  sound from eddies crossing the integration boundary (Wang, Lele & Moin, AIAA J. 34, 1996).
  This is cited via a search summary; the full text was not read.

### 4.4 §6G — lower the drag (0.08 → 0.01) to get a −3 slope

- **The diagnosis is right [cited].** Boffetta & Ecke (2012) §4.3: with linear friction "E(k)
  is predicted to become steeper than −3 with a correction proportional to the friction
  coefficient α" (Lilly 1972; Nam et al. 2000).
- **The fix is risky [cited].** Boffetta & Ecke §4.2: in a finite box without enough
  large-scale dissipation, energy accumulates at k_min into a condensate (Kraichnan 1967;
  Smith & Yakhot 1993–94; Chertkov et al. 2007, PRL 99, 084501).
- **Why that matters here.** The cone energy lives in the |k| = 1 shell [computed], so a
  condensate would change R drastically and worsen the box dependence.
- **Resolution may not allow −3 anyway [cited].** Borue (1993), as cited by Boffetta & Ecke,
  shows E(k) approaches k⁻³ only as resolution increases. At n = 128 a clean −3 may not be
  reachable whatever the drag.
- **Data rule.** A new dataset also conflicts with `CLAUDE.md` ("Don't regenerate datasets").
- **Recommendation:** keep the −3.98 slope, explain it with the friction citation, and drop
  the run.

### 4.5 "Do not implement cone_weighted_loss"

A diagnosis paper is much stronger with an intervention that tests whether the mechanism is
causal. Since the loss cannot see frequency (§4.2), the cheap intervention is a multi-step
(rollout) or time-derivative loss, not the cone-weighted loss. If B (and B_S) drop, the
mechanism is confirmed, and the open question at `README.md` survives.

### A.4 Far field: S-form vs T-form vs compact term (§1.3)

```python
import sys, numpy as np
sys.path.insert(0, '.')
from src.spectral import Grid
from src.solvers.io import load_dataset
from src.acoustics.lighthill import source_timeseries
from src.acoustics.greens import (source_spectrum, far_field_pressure,
                                  far_field_pressure_ibp, observer_ring)
u, v, meta = load_dataset('data/turb2d.npz')
g = Grid(n=u.shape[-1], length=meta['grid_length']); dt = meta['dt']
c0 = float(np.sqrt(np.mean(u**2 + v**2))) / 0.1
S = source_timeseries(u, v, g); sh, om = source_spectrum(S, dt); del S
th = {key: source_spectrum(a, dt)[0] for key, a in [('uu', u*u), ('uv', u*v), ('vv', v*v)]}
L = g.length; obs = observer_ring(30*L, 36, (L/2, L/2))
idx = sorted({int(np.argmin(abs(om - w))) for w in [1.5, 3, 6, 9, 11.3, 15, 25, 40, 60]})
pS = far_field_pressure(sh, om, g, obs, c0, True, idx)
pT = far_field_pressure_ibp(th, om, g, obs, c0, idx)
thm = {key: np.broadcast_to(a.mean(axis=(1, 2), keepdims=True), a.shape) for key, a in th.items()}
pC = far_field_pressure_ibp(thm, om, g, obs, c0, idx)     # compact (box-mean) term only
rms = lambda p: np.sqrt(np.mean(abs(p)**2))
for r, i in enumerate(idx):
    print(om[i], om[i]/c0, rms(pS[r]), rms(pT[r]), rms(pS[r])/rms(pT[r]), rms(pC[r])/rms(pT[r]))
```
